### Depth of field

`EnvironmentDOFParameters` had been carried through `EnvironmentSettings` and the inspector since Phase 7 opened, but nothing read `settings.depthOfField` and no pipeline existed - the four fields were fully wired to a dead end. Landed after the phase closed, not as a sub-phase of it, per the plan's own note that this was "a session or two whenever it is wanted."

**Technique: full-resolution, single-pass, CoC-weighted spiral gather**, put to the project owner as a choice against two alternatives (half-res with a guided upsample, and a two-pass separable blur) before writing any of it. The separable option was the one worth ruling out explicitly: a blur that can't reject background samples the way a disk gather can needs per-tap CoC weighting anyway to avoid haloing at depth edges, which erodes most of the cost saving it exists for. Recommended and chosen: one pass, ~32 taps on a Vogel/golden-angle spiral scaled to the pixel's own circle-of-confusion radius, each tap weighted by comparing *its own* CoC to how far it was sampled from - which is what stops a sharp point that happens to sit inside a wider blur radius sampled from elsewhere from smearing into it.

**The CoC formula came straight from the fields' own doc comments**, not from a separate design pass: `signedCoc = (viewDepth - focusPoint) / focusScale`, the near side additionally scaled by `nearScale`, then `saturate(abs(signedCoc)) * maxBlurSize`. `viewDepth` is the same camera-space depth every other screen-space pass already reconstructs - `dot(worldPos - cameraPosition, cameraForward)` off `worldPositionAt()`, not fog's radial distance, because a lens focuses on a plane perpendicular to its axis, not a sphere around it.

**Runs after bloom, in `PostChain`, exactly where the plan said it would**: a blurred bright highlight then spreads into the bokeh disk it would through a real lens, rather than bloom later re-sharpening what DOF already softened. It reads `_sceneDepth` as a shader resource, already established by fog and the screen-space passes.

**Writes into a target of its own instead of `_sceneColor` - deliberately not the pattern `reflections.psh` uses.** The reflection pass binds `_sceneColor` as both the shader resource and the render target in one draw, and it is safe only because a reflected ray, by construction, never lands back on the texel that cast it. A 32-tap disk gather has no such guarantee - many taps land well inside the frame's own already-shaded neighbourhood - so reading and writing the same texture here would make the result depend on GPU rasterization order. `PostChain` now owns `_dofTarget`, a second full scene-sized HDR texture, and `drawDepthOfField` returns whichever texture the composite should read: `sceneColor` untouched when the effect is off or has nothing to do, `_dofTarget` once it ran. No copy-back pass - the renderer forwards the returned pointer straight into `composite()`.

**Verification.**

- Enabled against the test scene's authored defaults (`focusPoint 10, focusScale 1, nearScale 1, maxBlurSize 20`) at first looked broken: the whole frame blurred uniformly, cluster included. It wasn't - the capsule/cube/scooter sit at camera-space depth ≈10-11, and a `focusScale` of 1 puts the in-focus band at ±1 unit, so the entire cluster already sits at the outer edge of it. Re-run with `focusScale` widened to 20 in the build directory's own config (not the source scene) produced exactly the expected gradient: the cluster sharp, the floor and sky progressively blurred with distance. Confirms the arithmetic; the shipped scene's own default is simply a shallow, close-focus setting nobody had tuned yet, which is authoring, not a bug, and was left alone.
- `diff.ps1`, disabled vs. enabled at the widened settings: 32.9% of pixels differ, max delta 182 - the effect is real and reaches most of the frame, as expected from a scene whose objects span a wide depth range.
- **Exact-zero check**: `maxBlurSize: 0` against `mode: Disabled` diffed to 10 of 3,504,640 pixels at max delta 1. Re-running the *disabled* config against itself, unchanged, produced the same 10 pixels at the same delta - a pre-existing capture-to-capture noise floor on this machine, not something the new code introduced. `drawDepthOfField`'s early-out (`mode == Disabled || maxBlurSize <= 0`) takes the identical code path either way: no resize, no draw call, `sceneColor` returned untouched.
- No `Diligent failed to compile` lines, no GL-state-guard warnings, on either run.
